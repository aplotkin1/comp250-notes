# Reflective Overview

For this note collection, I focused on how digital tools can help
students with learning, research, and organization. One of the main
questions I had while creating these notes was how technology can make
schoolwork easier without replacing the work that helps us actually
learn. I also wanted to look at how different tools can help with
different parts of academic work. The three sources I used focused on artificial intelligence, Zotero, and Git/GitHub. When reviewing the sources, I looked for passages that explained an important idea or made me question how I use technology. For the videos, timestamps were especially useful because they allowed me to return to specific explanations and verify my quotations.

Ethan Mollick's article was useful because it made me think more about
when AI should and should not be used. At first, I was mainly thinking
about how AI could save time or make difficult tasks easier. However,
the article brought my attention to some of the problems with relying
too much on AI. My notes about learning, hallucinations, effort, and
second opinions all connect to the idea that AI can be useful while
still requiring human judgment. I especially noticed the connection
between learning and effort because sometimes struggling with a problem
is part of understanding it.

The Zotero video moved my attention from AI to research organization.
Zotero showed me how technology can help with repetitive parts of
research without doing the actual thinking for the student. Features
such as collections and citation tools can make it easier to organize
sources and keep track of information. This connects with the Mollick
notes because both sources made me think about the difference between
using technology as assistance and allowing technology to replace my
own work.

The Git and GitHub video introduced another kind of organization.
Instead of organizing research sources, Git helps organize changes made
to files and projects. The ideas of commits, repositories, version
control, and branches showed me how a project can have a history instead
of only having one final version. This connects to Zotero because both
tools are designed to keep information organized and easier to return
to later. Across the notes, organization became a larger theme than I
originally expected.

I still have questions about how much students should depend on tools
like AI as they become more common in education. I also want to know
whether becoming dependent on tools that automate citations, writing,
or coding could make it harder to perform those tasks without the
tools. At the same time, these sources showed me that technology can be
very useful when the person using it understands its purpose and
limitations.

I used ChatGPT while creating this note collection. I used ChatGPT to help organize the structure of my notes, draft and revise commentary, identify connections between the three assigned sources, and format the collection consistently. I verified quotations against the assigned sources, including checking video quotations and timestamps myself. I did not treat AI responses as original source material. I think using AI in this way was useful because it helped with organization and revision while the assigned readings and videos remained the actual sources for my research.
