# Introduction

Generative artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly common in education, changing how students study, complete assignments, and understand difficult concepts. Tools such as ChatGPT can generate ideas, explanations, and offer academic assistance within seconds. Although these tools can be useful, they raise concerns about whether students are developing their own academic skills or becoming too dependent on technology. This issue is especially important because students at different educational levels have different learning needs and levels of independence. A tool that helps a college student understand a difficult concept might prevent a younger student from practicing the skills needed to solve problems independently. 

Research on generative AI in education identifies both potential benefits and risks. @alfarwan2025 looks at the use of generative AI in K-12 education, while @changrazi2026 identify concerns involving independent thinking and the reliability of AI-generated information among younger users. Meanwhile, @chencheung2025 report positive effects on university students' learning outcomes. These findings raise an important question about whether schools should apply the same AI policies to students of all ages or establish different rules based on students' educational levels.

Using the same AI policies across all educational levels could create problems. Allowing unrestricted AI use might encourage younger students to rely on generated answers before developing essential academic skills. However, banning AI entirely could prevent older students from benefiting from technology that is becoming increasingly relevant to education and future careers. Generative AI therefore requires different levels of regulation across education: stronger restrictions in K-8 to protect foundational learning, supervised and clearly defined use in high school, and responsible-use guidelines in higher education to balance academic integrity with the benefits of emerging technology.
