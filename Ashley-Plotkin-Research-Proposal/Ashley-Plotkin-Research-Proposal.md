## Part 1: Topic, Question, and Problem

My research topic is generative artificial intelligence in education, focusing on how its use should differ between elementary, middle school, high school, and college students. As generative AI becomes more common in schools, I want to examine whether it helps students learn or creates problems for their academic and personal development. My research question is: Should generative AI be allowed in higher education but restricted in K–12 education to protect students' learning and development? I believe college students should be allowed to use generative AI as long as universities establish clear rules about when and how it can be used. However, I believe K–8 students should face stronger restrictions because they are still developing important skills such as reading, writing, speaking, critical thinking, and problem-solving. High school students also raise concerns because relying too heavily on AI to complete homework, write essays, or answer questions could prevent them from developing the independent skills they need for college. I plan to use Chicago Public Schools (CPS) as a source to examine how its AI guidelines address academic honesty, student privacy, age-appropriate tools, and independent thinking. By examining CPS policies, academic research, and educational laws, I want to determine how schools can establish AI guidelines that protect student development while preparing students for a future where AI is increasingly common.
