# Note 7: AI and Higher-Order Thinking Skills

**Source:** Chen, Shuzhen, and Alan C. K. Cheung. 2025. “Effect of Generative Artificial Intelligence on University Students Learning Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.” *Educational Research Review* 49: 100737. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100737.

**Quotation:**

“Fourth, effect size for higher-order thinking was medium (g+ = 0.580, Q = 152.551, p < 0.000).” (Chen and Cheung 2025, 10)

**Commentary:**

I think generative AI can help college students develop critical-thinking and problem-solving skills, but it depends on what they ask it to do. For example, asking ChatGPT to explain a difficult concept or guide someone through a problem can help students understand the material better. However, asking AI to complete an assignment without making an effort to understand the answer could have the opposite effect. I believe students need to learn how to ask questions that encourage them to think rather than simply receive answers. This is why I support allowing AI in higher education with clear guidelines about responsible use. College students should be encouraged to use AI as a learning tool while still practicing their own reasoning and problem-solving skills. To me, the important issue is not just whether students use AI, but how they use it.
