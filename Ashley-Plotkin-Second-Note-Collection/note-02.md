# Note 2: AI Assistance vs. Actual Learning

**Source:** Alfarwan, Abdullah. 2025. “Generative AI Use in K-12 Education: A Systematic Review.” *Frontiers in Education* 10: 1647573. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1647573.

**Quotation:**

“the majority of studies have designed and developed self-reported questionnaires to assess their learning and understanding using self-perceptions, which may not be accurate measures.” (Alfarwan 2025, sec. 4.5)

**Commentary:**

I think one of the biggest concerns with generative AI in education is that students might believe they are learning when AI is actually doing most of the thinking for them. For example, a student could ask ChatGPT to solve a math problem and understand the explanation, but still struggle to solve a similar problem independently. I believe there is a difference between understanding an answer and knowing how to reach that answer yourself. This makes me question whether students' positive experiences with AI always reflect improvements in their actual learning. I think schools should test what students can accomplish without AI instead of relying only on how helpful students say the technology is. This is especially important for elementary students who are still developing independent learning skills.
