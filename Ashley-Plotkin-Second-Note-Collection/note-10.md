# Note 10: Fairness and Access to AI in Higher Education

**Source:** McGrath, Cormac, Alexandra Farazouli, and Teresa Cerratto-Pargman. 2025. “Generative AI Chatbots in Higher Education: A Review of an Emerging Research Area.” *Higher Education* 89: 1533–1549. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-024-01288-w.

**Quotation:**

“This includes being vigilant on issues regarding the potential inequity that GAI chatbots may cause among various student groups.” (McGrath, Farazouli, and Cerratto-Pargman 2025, 1546)

**Commentary:**

I think generative AI can create unfair advantages in college because some students can afford paid AI tools while others only have access to free versions. Students with more advanced tools might receive better explanations or additional help with their assignments. However, I do not think colleges should be responsible for providing paid AI subscriptions to every student. If a professor requires students to use a specific AI tool for an assignment, then I believe the school should make sure everyone has access to it without having to pay extra. Otherwise, students should be able to decide whether they want to use free or paid tools. I think the most important thing is that students have equal opportunities to complete required coursework. This connects to my research because responsible AI policies in higher education should consider fairness without requiring colleges to provide every available technology.
