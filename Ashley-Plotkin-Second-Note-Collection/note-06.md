# Note 6: Responsible AI Use in Higher Education

**Source:** Chen, Shuzhen, and Alan C. K. Cheung. 2025. “Effect of Generative Artificial Intelligence on University Students Learning Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.” *Educational Research Review* 49: 100737. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100737.

**Quotation:**

“First, the overall effect size of Gen-AI on students’ learning outcomes was large (g+ = 0.804, p < 0.000).” (Chen and Cheung 2025, 10)

**Commentary:**

I think generative AI can be a useful learning tool for college students when they use it responsibly. College students often have difficult assignments and challenging material to understand, so I believe AI can help by explaining complicated concepts, providing examples, and helping them prepare for exams. However, I think there is an important difference between using AI to understand an assignment and having AI complete the assignment for you. Students should still be responsible for their own work and demonstrate that they understand what they are learning. I also believe college students are generally better prepared than elementary students to recognize when AI is helpful and when they are becoming too dependent on it. This supports my argument that higher education should allow responsible AI use with clear academic honesty rules, while younger students need stronger restrictions.
