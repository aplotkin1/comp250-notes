# Note 8: Clear AI Guidelines in Higher Education

**Source:** McGrath, Cormac, Alexandra Farazouli, and Teresa Cerratto-Pargman. 2025. “Generative AI Chatbots in Higher Education: A Review of an Emerging Research Area.” *Higher Education* 89: 1533–1549. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-024-01288-w.

**Quotation:**

“the appropriate role of institutions is framed as to highlight the necessity of either harnessing the potential of GAI chatbots within HE or focusing on the urgency of adapting HE practices in order to safeguard learning and academic integrity.” (McGrath, Farazouli, and Cerratto-Pargman 2025, 1545)

**Commentary:**

I believe colleges should allow students to use generative AI, but professors need to establish clear guidelines about what is acceptable. For example, I think students should be allowed to use ChatGPT to brainstorm ideas, study for exams, or understand difficult concepts. However, submitting an assignment completely written by AI should not be considered acceptable because students would not be demonstrating their own understanding. I also think it is unfair when professors have different expectations but do not clearly communicate them to students. Without clear rules, students might accidentally misuse AI or become confused about what counts as academic dishonesty. In my opinion, colleges should focus on teaching students how to use AI responsibly rather than banning it entirely. This would allow students to benefit from the technology while protecting independent learning and academic integrity.
