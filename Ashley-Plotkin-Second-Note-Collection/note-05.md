# Note 5: Trusting AI Without Questioning It

**Source:** Chang, Tyler, and Afsaneh Razi. 2026. “A Systematic Literature Review of Generative AI Risks and Harms for Youth.” *Extended Abstracts of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*, article 54, 1–22. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798673.

**Quotation:**

“Youth often accepted outputs without verification [91], struggled to identify misinformation [39, 41, 101], and reported ambivalent ethical stances toward academic use [43, 70, 132].” (Chang and Razi 2026, 3)

**Commentary:**

I think younger students are more likely to believe information generated by AI without questioning whether it is accurate. Elementary students are still developing their critical-thinking skills, and they might assume that ChatGPT provides correct answers simply because its responses sound confident and convincing. This concerns me because students could learn incorrect information without realizing it. I also believe that relying on AI for answers could prevent younger students from learning how to evaluate sources and recognize misinformation independently. Although older students can also struggle with identifying inaccurate information, I think they generally have more experience questioning what they read. This strengthens my belief that elementary students should avoid using generative AI until they have developed stronger critical-thinking and information-evaluation skills.
