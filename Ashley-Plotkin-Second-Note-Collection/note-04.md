
# Note 4: AI Dependence and Independent Thinking

**Source:** Chang, Tyler, and Afsaneh Razi. 2026. “A Systematic Literature Review of Generative AI Risks and Harms for Youth.” *Extended Abstracts of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*, article 54, 1–22. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798673.

**Quotation:**

“while GAI could improve short-term performance [137, 143], it could also erode independent thinking [24, 44, 84], fragment ideation [30], and reduce retention [181, 183].” (Chang and Razi 2026, 3)

**Commentary:**

I find it concerning that generative AI could help students complete assignments faster while potentially weakening their ability to think independently. I believe students need to experience challenges and make mistakes because those experiences are an important part of learning. If students constantly rely on AI to generate answers or ideas, they might struggle when they have to complete assignments without it. This is especially concerning for elementary students who are still developing their foundational learning skills. Although AI might improve students' short-term academic performance, I don't think completing assignments more efficiently necessarily means they are developing a deeper understanding of the material. I think schools need to consider students' long-term learning rather than focusing only on immediate academic results.
